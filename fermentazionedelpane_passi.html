Alcoholic Fermentation in Bread Making

Didactic Note (Upper Secondary School - 2nd Biennium): The following procedure explains the biochemical process of Saccharomyces cerevisiae using Simplified Technical English (ASD-STE100) principles (active voice, short sentences, approved vocabulary, and one instruction per step) adapted for CLIL/science education.

  1. Step 1: Hydration and Activation Mix the flour, the water, and the yeast (Saccharomyces cerevisiae). The water activates the enzymes in the flour and the yeast cells.
  2. Step 2: Starch Breakdown The amylase enzymes in the flour break down the complex starch molecules into simple sugars, such as maltose and glucose.
  3. Step 3: Glycolysis (Anaerobic Phase) When the oxygen in the dough decreases, the yeast absorbs the glucose. Inside the cytoplasm, the yeast converts one glucose molecule into two pyruvate molecules. This process produces a small amount of energy (2 ATP).
  4. Step 4: Decarboxylation of Pyruvate The enzyme pyruvate decarboxylase removes a carboxyl group from each pyruvate molecule. This chemical reaction releases carbon dioxide gas (CO2) and forms acetaldehyde.
  5. Step 5: Reduction to Ethanol The enzyme alcohol dehydrogenase reduces the acetaldehyde to ethanol (ethyl alcohol). This step regenerates the NAD+ coenzyme, which allows glycolysis to continue.
  6. Step 6: Dough Leavening The gluten network in the dough traps the carbon dioxide bubbles. The gas bubbles expand and cause the volume of the dough to increase.
  7. Step 7: Baking and Thermal Inactivation Put the dough in a hot oven. The heat expands the gas bubbles further, evaporates the ethanol, and kills the yeast cells at approximately 55–60 °C.
C6H12O6 (Glucose) → 2 C2H5OH (Ethanol) + 2 CO2 (Carbon Dioxide) + 2 ATP
